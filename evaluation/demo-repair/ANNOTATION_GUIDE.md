# Fan boundary review protocol

The current Roboflow masks are weak labels produced from Planet Four wedges and a dark-pixel threshold. They often cover the darkest core and omit a diffuse fringe. The 24 chips in `annotation-queue.json` are a **pending manual review queue**, not corrected data. They span the three training observations and sparse through dense images. Do not train a replacement segmentation model from this queue until each used chip is marked complete.

For each chip, inspect the original pixels at native scale and annotate **every visible fan**, including ones missing from the old masks. Mark the continuous deposit body from its narrow source through the visible widening plume. Include a faint fringe only where its boundary is distinguishable from the surrounding terrain; use the same contrast convention across the batch. Keep adjacent deposits separate when their sources or plume bodies can be distinguished. Do not draw a tight mask around only the dark core. A shadow, dark spot, seam, rock texture, or unresolved gradient is not a fan mask. Record ambiguous regions in review notes rather than silently assigning a positive or negative label.

Before marking a chip complete, inspect the whole chip for missed fans, inspect each mask over the source pixels with fill temporarily hidden, and record whether diffuse edges were included consistently. A second reviewer should spot-check the most ambiguous chips and all proposed negatives. Split future model comparisons by HiRISE observation. Keep `ESP_092344_0970` for hard-negative evaluation and reserve another unseen observation for a later final check; its three current crops are not expert-labeled.

Required per-chip review record: image name; reviewer; date; fan count; explicit complete-chip confirmation; uncertain-region notes; and whether the original weak masks clipped plumes, merged fans, or covered non-fans. A chip without that confirmation must not be used as a negative example merely because it has no annotations.
